Who this is for

This short series takes you from "what is AI, really?" to a real service you can sell, with an AI helper doing the heavy lifting. Each one is a 5 to 10 minute read, written for people who run a business or a practice, not for programmers.

By the end you will be able to:

  • Explain the vocabulary — AI, LLM, prompt, context, tools, MCP, skills, agents, agentic workflows — in your own words, to a client or a colleague.
  • Sketch an agent for one specific task in your work: what it reads, what it can do, what it knows, and where a person checks its output.
  • Shape a service around that agent: what you sell, how it is delivered, how you price it and how you earn a client's trust.

The series stays away from brand names and from engineering detail. Where an idea has a shape — a loop, a flow, a hierarchy — it is drawn rather than described.

One example runs through every post. Linh is an accountant with a small practice. Each month her clients send her a pile of receipts and invoices, and her team types them into spreadsheets, sorts them into categories and chases the missing ones. She wants to turn that chore into a service she can sell to many more small businesses, with an AI agent doing most of the work and her team checking it. We follow her from first idea to first paying client.

The reading path

Posts 1 to 5 each add one ingredient. Post 6 combines them into an agent, Post 7 arranges agents and people into a workflow, Posts 8 and 9 build it and sell it, and Post 10 looks at the business models that are working.

Read the posts in order the first time: each one uses the words the earlier ones explained. After that, the glossary is the quickest way back to a term.
